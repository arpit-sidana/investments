**My preferred portfolio combines established AI distribution with selected physical bottlenecks. The best modeled upside is in Uber, Intuit, GoDaddy and Fabrinet; the strongest infrastructure anchors are Nvidia, TSMC and Broadcom.** This is a different opportunity set from simply buying the next component that has already rallied.

- The proposed **$100k portfolio holds 21 stocks and $8k cash**. Its modeled expected terminal value is about **$135k**, before dividends, taxes, fees and cash interest. The synchronized operating bear case is about **$60k**; losses can exceed that.
- Your K-shaped framework identifies useful opportunities, but several stronger claims do not survive the evidence. Housing bargaining power has shifted toward buyers, while bank-card spending growth has broadened across income groups. Airbnb’s reported business was growing, rather than collapsing.
- The data-collection opportunity is real in principle. The more durable public-market opportunity is often **control of a recurring workflow and permissioned data**, plus an existing customer relationship. A one-time dataset sale and a repeatable, high-margin revenue stream deserve different valuations.

The model gives memory and optical components substantial downside even where demand is strong. It also gives software incumbents explicit disruption cases: an installed customer base is valuable only if it retains the customer, execution rights and economic margin.

## The K-shaped economy is useful, but it is not a single trade

**Separate wealth, income growth, affordability and spending behavior.** Asset owners can gain wealth while wage growth converges; a household can feel poorer despite increasing nominal spending. Age, income, homeownership, geography, debt and family formation are different dimensions.

The most relevant current evidence is mixed:

| Claim | What the evidence supports | Investment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation is still a constraint | September CPI rose 3.7% year over year and 0.6% month over month; gasoline rose 9.1% in the month. | Do not assume rapid rate cuts or automatic valuation expansion. |
| Lower-income consumption is collapsing | Bank of America reported September nominal card-spending growth of 6.2%, or 5.1% excluding gasoline, and broader growth across income cohorts. Its August report still identified unusually strong top-5% spending. | A wealth divide can coexist with improving lower-income spending growth. |
| Credit stress is spreading uncontrollably | New York Fed Q2 debt was $18.8T; 4.7% was delinquent. Credit-card serious-delinquency transitions were elevated but roughly steady. | Monitor deterioration; do not infer an imminent aggregate consumption crash from high debt balances alone. |
| Housing is a buyer’s market | Redfin estimated 62.6% more sellers than buyers in September, with 41 of 50 large metros classified as buyer’s markets. | Transaction frictions, repair/remodel activity and local price concessions can persist. |
| All sub-$1M homes are crashing; all $5M+ homes are booming | The sources do not establish those nationwide price cutoffs. Redfin’s luxury definition and metro mix differ. | Require local inventory, completed transactions and price-tier data before making a housing trade. |
| Travel and Airbnb are collapsing | Airbnb reported Q2 revenue growth of 17% and Nights and Seats Booked growth of 10%. Delta reported broad September demand strength. | Weak individual hosts or mass-market travel cohorts do not establish platform-wide collapse. |

Sources: [BLS September CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm), [October consumer spending](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/economic-insights/consumer-checkpoint-october-2026.html), [August income-cohort discussion](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/economic-insights/consumer-checkpoint-august-2026.html), [New York Fed household credit](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260811/), [Redfin housing balance](https://www.redfin.com/news/buyers-vs-sellers-september-2026/), [Airbnb Q2](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-q2-2026-financial-results), [Delta Q3](https://ir.delta.com/news/news-details/2026/Delta-Air-Lines-Announces-September-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx).

The Fed raised its target range to **3.75%–4.00% in September**, explicitly citing elevated inflation. That supports a financing constraint, not certainty that rates must remain unchanged through 2027. [September FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm).

### How human behavior changes the opportunity

Small affordable treats can satisfy convenience, reward and social participation when larger aspirations feel unattainable. That is a plausible hypothesis for some consumers, not verified proof that people order delivery because they have abandoned homeownership. Time scarcity, dual-income schedules, disability, promotions and subscription economics can produce the same behavior.

The investable distinction is **frequency × contribution profit × retention**. More orders driven by subsidies can destroy shareholder value. A $30 delivery order is not equivalent to $30 of platform revenue, and platform revenue is not equivalent to free cash flow.

At the wealthy end, customers buy time, access, certainty and status. AI can make discovery and coordination cheaper; scarce restaurant tables, trusted human service and desirable locations can retain pricing power. An $80 matcha or a private-club waitlist is evidence of a niche willingness to pay, not an estimate of market size.

A useful segmentation for this portfolio is:

- **Financially constrained:** lower prices, essential goods, lower administrative costs and value-for-money services. AI beneficiaries include SMB tools that save real labor; merchant failure rates remain the counterweight.
- **Stable earners with limited time:** trusted automation, accounting, payments, travel and convenience. Distribution and integration matter more than model novelty.
- **Affluent asset owners:** premium experiences, privacy, access and concierge service. The wealth effect helps demand, but a stock-market downturn can reverse both spending and advertising.
- **Businesses facing labor and financing pressure:** tools that recover revenue, reduce errors or complete tasks with little implementation effort. This is the strongest cross-income AI adoption mechanism.

These are economic hypotheses to test against cohorts and unit economics, not demographic stereotypes.

## Follow the path from a useful task to retained profit

A useful AI demonstration becomes investable when someone has a budget, the system can act reliably, the customer keeps paying, and the provider retains profit after inference, support, distribution and capital costs.

The practical sequence is:

1. **A costly task exists.** Missed restaurant bookings, unreconciled accounts, slow insurance estimates, customer-service queues or underused compute.
2. **The provider has context and permission to act.** Records, payment credentials, workflow integrations, audit trails and an escalation path.
3. **Adoption is easy enough.** Existing distribution, a familiar invoice and measurable ROI can beat a technically superior product requiring a new implementation.
4. **The payment model captures the value.** A seat, transaction, completed task or premium subscription must produce incremental gross profit.
5. **Competition and capital spending leave a return for shareholders.** Falling model costs can help customers while competition gives away the savings.

| Layer | What the customer pays for | Public candidates | What must be proven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compute and fabrication | Useful deployed capacity | NVDA, TSM, AVGO | Utilization, customer returns, cash collection |
| Memory and connectivity | Bandwidth, reliability, working capacity | MU, SK hynix, FN, COHR, CRDO | Yield, qualification, pricing and supply discipline |
| Power and cooling | Operational racks delivered on time | VRT, ETN | Accepted projects and cash-generating backlog |
| Enterprise workflows | Work completed with controls | MSFT, NOW, CRM, INTU, ADBE | Paid renewals after inference and lost seats |
| Trusted professional data | Decisions that can be defended | TRI, RELX, VRSK | Incremental paid usage and retained authority |
| SMB go-to-market | More customers and less administration | GDDY, TOST | Paid conversion, lower churn and merchant ROI |
| Consumer distribution and advertising | Matching demand to supply | UBER, META, GOOGL, AMZN, RDDT | Incremental contribution profit and retention |

These exposures overlap. Microsoft can benefit from agent adoption while paying Nvidia, and those revenues are not independent sources of portfolio diversification.

**My strongest general thesis is that cheaper intelligence raises the value of distribution, trusted context and execution.** It can also reduce the price of software features and generic data. Owning records is useful only when the business has the right to use them and can convert that advantage into a paid product.

## The physical bottleneck changes as architectures and supply change

Memory and storage were logical beneficiaries of rising AI workloads. They were not guaranteed “easy money”: at each entry price the investor still had to forecast supply, qualification, pricing, financing and what the market already expected. A correct industry forecast can produce a poor stock return if profits normalize or the entry multiple is too high.

### Memory: distinguish structural demand from a pricing cycle

Training and inference need different mixes of HBM, conventional DRAM, server low-power memory and storage. Longer-running agents can expand working context and persistent state. But better caching, quantization, compression, routing and memory sharing can reduce hardware required per task. Lower cost can stimulate usage; the net effect on supplier revenue is an empirical question.

Micron’s unusually high reported margins make **normalized earnings** decisive. The model’s $110 base EPS is below annualized near-term guidance, yet still assumes extraordinary industry profitability. SK hynix’s reported net profit exceeded revenue in Q2; operating earnings and cash collection are more useful anchors than headline net income. [Micron Q4](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/), [SK hynix Q2](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/).

Samsung’s current HBM4 and HBM4E progress means competition cannot be omitted from a scarcity thesis. Watch qualification, yield and conventional-memory supply alongside customer agreements. Agreements improve visibility, but their pricing, volume flexibility and credit terms determine protection. [Samsung Q2 results](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-second-quarter-2026-results).

### Optics: CPO is a change in value capture, not a universal buy signal

CPO places optical engines near switching or compute silicon. It can reduce electrical distance and power, but adds packaging, thermal, test, serviceability and production challenges. Pluggable modules, near-packaged optics, external lasers and short-reach copper can coexist across distances and architectures.

Nvidia already described Spectrum-6 systems supporting **both pluggable and co-packaged optics** in its August release. Lumentum announced an external-laser module with initial availability expected in **1H2027**. Those are concrete product references; neither establishes that all network connections become CPO by 2027. [Nvidia earnings](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027), [Lumentum product announcement](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-to-Demonstrate-DWDM-ELSFP-Laser-Module-for-OCI-MSA-Applications-at-ECOC-2026/default.aspx).

The relevant competitive questions differ:

- **Fabrinet:** can qualified manufacturing scale across customer designs, and does volume growth retain attractive margins?
- **Coherent/Lumentum:** who owns qualified laser and optical-component capacity, and how much system value remains with them?
- **Credo/Astera:** which electrical, DSP, retimer and rack-connectivity sockets survive or expand as distances and architectures change?
- **Arista/Broadcom/Nvidia:** who controls switching silicon, systems, software and the customer relationship?
- **AOI and other module makers:** can announced capacity become qualified, profitable shipments before supply catches up?

Optical demand growth can coincide with falling average selling prices. In-source designs can move profit toward hyperscalers or silicon vendors. The portfolio therefore owns several distinct capabilities but limits their combined size.

### Power and deployment may monetize before new model features

A rack awaiting power or commissioning produces no useful tokens. Eaton and Vertiv address deliverability and operation, not simply model performance. Their demand can be more architecture-agnostic, but it is still exposed to customer capex, permitting, grid constraints and project financing.

The next bottleneck could be deployment labor, testing, power electronics, data access or customer integration. I would buy an identified order-to-profit mechanism, not rotate automatically from the last winner into the nearest adjacent component.

## The data-broker opportunity is real; recurring economics are the harder part

Mercor publicly offers to license company workflow data, process it and pay the business. Its stated inputs include communications, code and business-system records, and its payout depends on volume and depth. That verifies the existence of a market-facing business model. It does **not** independently verify buyer budgets, broker profits, typical seller payouts, or the claim that visiting small restaurants and salons routinely generates millions per month. [Mercor Data](https://www.mercor.com/data/).

The unverified viral claim combines several different businesses:

| Business | Potential source of value | Main economic weakness |
|---|---|---|
| One-time archive licensing | Scarce records of real work | Finite inventory; nonrecurring revenue |
| Data brokerage/referrals | Finding sellers and arranging access | Buyer concentration; low switching costs |
| Cleaning and labeling | Quality, de-identification and expert evaluation | Labor cost, procurement pressure, automation |
| Agent environments and evaluation | Realistic tasks and measurable outcomes | Expensive maintenance; specialized expertise |
| Live workflow software | Ongoing context, permissions and execution | Integration costs and incumbent competition |
| Professional decision products | Trusted data plus domain-specific output | Interface disintermediation and liability |

A useful private-company diligence checklist is therefore commercial: named and repeat buyers, recognized revenue versus bookings, gross margin after expert labor, repeat-license rights, concentration, retention, collections, and the cost of replacing a sold dataset. Payment for one large archive is not monthly recurring revenue.

Data access also has contractual and privacy constraints. De-identification is one processing step; the investment still depends on usable rights, permitted purposes and enforceable customer relationships. The report assumes no unauthorized resale of merchant, employee or patient records.

For public equities, **TRI, RELX and VRSK** offer existing professional-data economics. **INTU, TOST and CRM** offer useful workflow context and distribution, without any assumption that they sell customer records to labs. **RDDT** has a direct data-access angle, but its reported advertising business is much larger than Other revenue. These are distinct mechanisms, not interchangeable “data plays.”

Private companies such as Mercor, Scale AI, Surge AI, Handshake, Harvey and vertical-agent startups are competitive context, not securities available in this public-equity portfolio. No current private valuation or ownership status is assumed here. A small private operator can have exceptional economics that are immaterial inside a large public parent; proxy exposure must be checked for size and terms.

## Luxury and small treats matter through distribution and unit economics

Your consumer observations are useful leads. To turn them into a stock thesis, identify which company owns supply, the customer relationship, the payment and the repeat purchase.

**Premium access and concierge.** The value is often a scarce reservation, a trusted operator or a human who solves a difficult problem. AI can lower the cost of searching, planning and coordination, while making generic concierge chat easier to copy. Public candidates for a separate affluent-spending portfolio include American Express, Booking Holdings, premium hotel managers, luxury brands and selected travel operators. Their returns require card, occupancy, fee, credit and valuation analysis; the AI angle alone does not earn them a top-25 place here.

American Express’s investor materials identify its premium-lifestyle positioning and a proposed TheFork acquisition. That supports a distribution/access strategy, not a conclusion that every reservation is oversubscribed or that AI is its primary profit driver. [American Express investor releases](https://ir.americanexpress.com/news/investor-relations-news/default.aspx).

Aman, Blacklane, private clubs and an “Atlas” concierge-card anecdote should not be mapped to a ticker without verifying the exact entity, ownership and economics. This report does not treat them as stand-alone public equities or assume public shareholders own material exposure to them.

**Convenience and affordable rewards.** Uber and Toast can benefit from habitual demand and better merchant operations. The useful AI link is improved matching, marketing, support and order economics. There is no verified causal evidence here that abandoning homeownership is the main reason for delivery spending. DoorDash is a relevant competitor and candidate, but the thesis must survive fulfillment costs, merchant fees, promotions and valuation.

**Value-oriented consumption.** Walmart, Costco, TJX, Ross and other value retailers can benefit from trade-down or convenience. AI may improve inventory, labor planning and retail advertising. This is a separate margin/distribution thesis; a defensive business can still have an unattractive stock price. Dollar stores are not automatic winners when their customers are under pressure.

**Healthcare, insurance and essential services.** Documentation, scheduling, coding, claims and collections can create measurable AI savings. The constraint is trusted integration and accountability, not a lack of possible use cases. Verisk supplies one mature public route. Healthcare-service companies need reimbursement, utilization and liability analysis before AI efficiency can be credited to shareholders.

These broader areas inform the opportunity map, but have not all received a full three-scenario valuation. They are **candidates for comparison**, not rejected stocks proven to have lower expected returns.

## The 2027 catalysts are proof points, not just product launch dates

The company analyses distinguish announced events from **analyst underwriting milestones**. A reporting season is an opportunity for evidence, not a promised share-price catalyst.

| Window | What is likely to change the investment case | Evidence to demand |
|---|---|---|
| End-2026 | Guidance for 2027, infrastructure order conversion, paid AI renewals and capital budgets | Organic growth, committed versus recognized revenue, cash collection, cost to serve |
| 1H2027 | Tax-season economics; enterprise renewal cohorts; 1.6T/HBM/customer qualifications; electrical transaction milestones | Net retention, paid tasks, yields, utilization, accepted shipments, gross profit |
| 2H2027 / year-end | Whether the first large deployment cohorts produce durable profit | FCF after capex and leases, dilution-adjusted EPS, renewal behavior, pricing and supply discipline |

Concrete references include **GoDaddy’s December 1, 2026 Investor Night**, **Vertiv’s October 28, 2026 earnings**, **Lumentum’s expected 1H2027 external-laser availability**, and **Eaton’s expected Q1 2027 Mobility separation**. The latter two remain management expectations, not guaranteed dates. [GoDaddy filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609711/000160971126000087/gddyex991-20260630xq2earni.htm), [Vertiv date](https://investors.vertiv.com/news/news-details/2026/Vertiv-Announces-Date-of-Third-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Release-and-Conference-Call/), [Lumentum release](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-to-Demonstrate-DWDM-ELSFP-Laser-Module-for-OCI-MSA-Applications-at-ECOC-2026/default.aspx), [Eaton results](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/news-insights/news-releases/2026/eaton-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results.html).

**My priorities by window:** End-2026 favors earnings and guidance checks in INTU, GDDY, NVDA, AVGO and FN. By mid-2027, INTU's tax season, NOW/CRM renewals and FN/COHR/CRDO qualifications should provide more operating evidence. By end-2027, the deciding measure across MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META and the agent vendors is cash profit after the infrastructure and service bill. Memory stays conditional on pricing and cash collection throughout; a calendar date alone does not justify increasing it.

For agents, the important inflection is **pilot → paid deployment → repeat use → renewal → profit**. Revenue can lag bookings, and profit can lag revenue. For hardware, it is **design win → qualification → volume shipment → acceptance → cash**. Market prices can anticipate each stage well before the income statement.

An agent that reduces labor cost is not automatically additive for its software vendor. Fewer seats, outcome-based pricing and competition can transfer the savings to the customer. Likewise, a component supplier can ship more bits or ports and earn less if pricing normalizes.

### 1. Uber (UBER)

**Oct 16 close:** $70.35 (USD) · **Modeled expected return:** +61.4% · **Portfolio weight:** 7% · **Entry/base EPS:** 15.6×. [Historical-price source](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/uber/history/).

**Investment mechanism.** The scarce asset is aggregated demand, payments, dispatch and local supply. AI can improve matching and support before autonomy is economically material. The strongest version of the thesis is that autonomous fleets need distribution; it is not that Uber must win vehicle manufacturing.

**Reported starting point.** Q2 gross bookings were $58.0B, trips grew 18%, normalized EPS was $0.81 and trailing free cash flow exceeded $10B. GAAP EPS of $1.17 included investment gains. [Earnings / filing](https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2026/Uber-Announces-Results-for-Second-Quarter-2026/default.aspx) (dated 2026-08-05).

**Base earnings bridge — analyst assumptions.** CY2027 gross bookings of about $285B × 3.2% normalized net-income conversion ÷ 2.03B diluted shares ≈ $4.50 EPS. This requires roughly 23% growth over the Q2 bookings run rate and a modest conversion improvement.

| Case / probability | CY2027 EPS | End-2027 P/E | End-2027 price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear / 30% | $2.60 | 16× | $41.60 |
| Base / 50% | $4.50 | 28× | $126.00 |
| Bull / 20% | $5.60 | 34× | $190.40 |

**Bear.** Autonomous operators retain customers, insurance and driver costs rise, and delivery competition forces incentives; $2.60 EPS and a 16× multiple.

**Base.** Demand and cross-platform usage grow; dispatch, advertising and operating efficiency improve earnings without requiring a mass robotaxi rollout.

**Bull.** Autonomous partnerships expand supply while Uber retains distribution economics; $5.60 EPS and 34× require strong booking growth and confidence in durable take rates.

**2026–27 catalysts and proof points**

- End-2026: Q3/Q4 booking growth, EPS conversion and autonomous-partner unit economics.
- Mid-2027: evidence that autonomous markets add incremental profitable trips rather than subsidized volume.
- End-2027: repeat usage, buybacks and normalized earnings establish whether the marketplace remains the customer interface.

**Change the thesis if:** Two quarters of bookings growth below 12% with deteriorating normalized operating margin, or demonstrable loss of customer ownership to autonomous fleets.

**Valuation and risk.** High disruption uncertainty. Premium rides are only one use case; this is not a pure luxury-consumer investment.

**Evidence confidence:** Medium. **Earnings definition:** Normalized non-GAAP; remove investment revaluations and unusual tax items.

## How to challenge the valuation model

Every company has three **end-2027 price scenarios**, each built from an explicit **calendar-2027 normalized diluted EPS × terminal P/E**. The earnings bridge shows an indicative revenue, after-tax margin and diluted-share combination supporting the base EPS. These are analyst assumptions, not company forecasts or a complete integrated financial statement model.

**Expected terminal price = bear probability × bear price + base probability × base price + bull probability × bull price. Expected price return = expected terminal price ÷ October 16 close − 1.** SK hynix additionally applies the stated KRW translation assumptions to each price ratio.

The probabilities sum to 100% for each company. They are judgments about business outcomes, informed by concentration, cyclicality and execution, not empirically calibrated frequencies. A 35% bear weight does not mean a measured 35% chance of any share-price loss. The three endpoints do not describe the intervening trading path.

### Why these earnings and multiples

The model values calendar-2027 earnings at year-end 2027. It does **not** silently combine FY2027 earnings with a 2028 target horizon. Fiscal-year reports are converted into calendar-year assumptions; Intuit and Nvidia are examples where the year label matters.

- **Compute platforms:** base multiples around 25–31× require durable growth, high returns and strong customer economics; bear multiples of 17–22× allow digestion and competitive pressure.
- **Memory:** 9–11× normalized base earnings reflects cycle risk. Applying a premium growth multiple to peak scarcity earnings would double-count optimism.
- **Connectivity:** manufacturing and component cases receive different multiples. Fabrinet's 25× base reflects qualified manufacturing growth; Credo's 40× requires a much longer high-growth runway and is paired with a 35% bear probability.
- **Workflow and data businesses:** base multiples of roughly 17–35× depend on retention, pricing, growth and cash conversion. Adobe and GoDaddy receive lower multiples than ServiceNow because disruption and growth differ.
- **Consumer distribution:** Uber's 28× base and Reddit's 30× base require profitable growth. Their bear probabilities reflect autonomy and traffic/interface risk.

The **entry price/base EPS** column is a model comparison, not the quoted consensus forward P/E. Different earnings definitions prevent a mechanically comparable cross-sector ranking by P/E alone.

### Accounting choices that can change the answer

Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia, Uber and Salesforce have investment-related or unusual earnings items requiring normalization. The report does not annualize those gains. Intuit's FY2027 non-GAAP presentation includes SBC; comparing it directly with older adjusted figures would be misleading. ServiceNow prices and shares reflect its 5-for-1 split. [Intuit filing](https://investors.intuit.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000896878-26-000029/fy26q4earningspressrelease.htm), [ServiceNow split filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1373715/000137371525000321/now-20251205.htm).

Several models use company-style adjusted earnings that exclude SBC and amortization. Modeled diluted share counts and lower multiples limit optimism, but **they do not fully expense SBC or eliminate the accounting gap**. Review GAAP earnings, buybacks net of employee issuance and FCF after capex before implementing those cases.

Net margins are after interest and tax, so debt is not subtracted again from a P/E-derived equity price. Balance-sheet risk still affects earnings and multiples. Current capex, leases and working capital can make FCF much lower than EPS. The model is not a DCF and does not prove the reinvestment earns its cost of capital.

RELX uses an assumed GBP/USD 1.30 in its base earnings bridge. SK hynix uses 10% KRW depreciation in bear, unchanged FX in base and 5% appreciation in bull; its ranking reflects USD return. TSM ADR EPS is already modeled in USD. Other global exposures still carry operating FX and geopolitical risk.

Dividends, cash yield, taxes, fees, withholding, spreads and future splits are excluded from returns. Eaton transaction consideration is also not separately valued; its scenario is less precise until terms and post-transaction guidance are incorporated. No options or leverage are assumed.

### What the model cannot establish

The three-point distribution omits explicit bankruptcy/conflict endpoints and may overstate EV if tails are underweighted. As a simple sensitivity, assigning 5% probability to zero and rescaling the original scenarios to 95% would reduce each expected terminal price by 5%. For the proposed portfolio this reduces expected price return by about 6.3 percentage points.

Small changes in probability, margins or exit multiples can reorder adjacent ranks. Do not treat a 25.9% expected return as meaningfully different from 25.7%. The table's precision makes arithmetic inspectable; it does not make beliefs precise.

No YTD return, prior peak or momentum extrapolation enters the target calculation. A lower share price matters only in relation to a defensible future earnings distribution.

## Implement the thesis with exposure limits and cash

The portfolio below is a **hypothetical $100k growth allocation at the October 16 prices**, with 92% equities and 8% cash. Dollar allocations are budget weights, not rounded executable share orders. Fractional availability, taxes and broker access must be checked at implementation.

Weights deliberately differ from raw EV ranks. Nvidia, TSMC, Microsoft and Amazon receive larger weights for broader franchises and stronger evidence; Reddit receives only 1% because its traffic and monetization assumptions are less secure. Multiple customer-facing platforms provide different ways to monetize AI, but all remain exposed to the same economy.

Zero-weight watchlist names are **RELX, VRSK, ETN and SK hynix**. They can replace overlapping holdings if the evidence or prices improve. Zero weight is not a negative company judgment.

### Entry discipline

Allocate approximately **half of each intended equity position initially**, another quarter after the next earnings release confirms the thesis, and the final quarter on either a valuation improvement with intact fundamentals or a second confirming result. This sequence manages implementation uncertainty; it is not a claim that market timing increases expected returns.

The intended full equity budget is $92k, so the first tranche is about $46k; uncommitted funds initially remain cash. The displayed terminal portfolio assumes the full target allocation at the reference prices, not a simulated staged-entry strategy. Actual staged fills will differ.

The model's **25%-return entry ceiling** is expected terminal price ÷ 1.25. It is a scenario-dependent discipline tool, not fair value or a guaranteed margin of safety. It is especially useful when a stock rallies before purchase. For names already above that ceiling, reassess the position's quality/diversification role or wait for better evidence.

Cap an individual target position at 8%; keep the most speculative connectivity and consumer cases small. Do not double an impaired position merely because it is down. Reassess each position after earnings and after a material change in customer concentration, competitive architecture or financing.

### Portfolio scenarios and uncertainty

- **Probability-weighted expected value:** about **$135k** at end-2027, a **35%** price return.
- **All-company base endpoints:** about **$143k**, a **43%** price return.
- **All-company operating bear endpoints:** about **$60k**, a **40%** loss.
- **All-company bull endpoints:** about **$222k**, a **122%** gain.

The synchronized endpoints are stress illustrations, not portfolio scenario probabilities. Company outcome probabilities differ; there is no defensible single 25/55/20 portfolio distribution here. Expected value adds across positions without requiring independence, but loss probabilities and drawdowns require a joint model that has not been estimated.

Moving **10 percentage points of probability from bull to bear for every holding** reduces modeled expected portfolio return by about **16 percentage points**, to roughly **18.5%**. That sensitivity is a more useful check on optimism than the headline expected return alone.

An across-the-board 60% equity decline would leave about **$44.8k** including cash. Taiwan disruption, a funding shock or severe recession can be worse than the ordinary operating bear assumptions.

### Risks that are shared across apparently different holdings

The portfolio has about **20% direct compute exposure, 7% connectivity, 2% memory and 3% power/cooling**. The remainder is primarily customer distribution, workflows and data. These categories still share hyperscaler budgets, foundry capacity, electricity, equity wealth and enterprise employment.

A K-shaped economy creates a feedback loop: AI-related equity wealth can support premium consumption and ad spending; a stock-market decline can weaken both while tightening infrastructure funding. “AI plus luxury” is therefore not automatically a hedge.

Midterm outcomes are a scenario variable. A known election date does not prove that policy outcomes, export controls, tariffs, taxes, procurement or power regulation are fully priced. I apply no directional election premium. Higher yields, expensive energy and sticky inflation pressure long-duration valuations and infrastructure financing even when demand remains strong.

The operating response is to preserve cash, insist on collections and useful customer outcomes, and resize exposure when the evidence changes. A growth portfolio should still avoid being forced to sell during a financing or earnings shock.

## What would make me change the portfolio

Review the same small set of evidence each quarter. These are decision rules, not an automated trading instruction.

| Test | Evidence strengthening the thesis | Evidence weakening it |
|---|---|---|
| End-customer AI ROI | Paid renewals, lower cost per completed task, higher useful utilization | Rising usage with weak conversion or persistent subsidies |
| Hardware demand quality | Accepted shipments, collections, stable inventory and customer returns | Receivables/inventory outrun sales; orders shift without cash |
| Memory/optical pricing | Qualified supply and mix support profit despite more volume | ASP and margins fall faster than bit/port demand grows |
| Software value capture | AI wallet expansion exceeds lost seats and inference cost | Rebranded ARR, acquired growth or activity counts mask weak organic revenue |
| Consumer behavior | Cohort retention and contribution profit improve | Promotions drive transactions while profitability deteriorates |
| Financing | Capex funded by durable customer cash generation | Vendor/customer financing and equity issuance sustain uneconomic demand |
| K-shaped macro | Affordability stabilizes without a collapse in asset wealth | Credit deterioration, unemployment and wealth declines reinforce one another |

**Increase conviction** when revenue, gross profit and cash tell the same story. **Reduce conviction** when management changes metric definitions, highlights total activity without paid economics, or needs increasingly generous financing to maintain growth.

The largest unresolved questions are the durability of scarcity margins, who owns the agent interface, and how much incremental AI profit is left after capex and stock compensation. Those questions determine whether the model’s base cases are conservative or optimistic.

The ranking is most useful as a set of falsifiable investment theses. It should be updated when prices, filings or customer economics change; it should not be defended simply because the AI story remains exciting.
